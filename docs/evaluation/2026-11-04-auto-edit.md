# Auto in the editor reads the photograph first (2026-11-04)

**The owner's request.** On a dark portrait the automatic enhancement made a grey background; the owner then set the sliders by hand (brightness -65, contrast 18, shadows -18, highlights 1, saturation 14,
warmth 9, noise reduction 41, deblur 32, face restoration 37) and got the look they wanted. Auto in the editor returned **no change** for that photograph (every slider 0), and "brighten" lifted it
further. They asked for Auto to look at the photograph, understand what it is, and set the sliders itself, "like I have edit".

## What was measured (the owner's photograph, 472 x 708; L is CIE lightness on the renderer's 0-255 scale)

| | black floor (median of the dark mass) | 95th percentile (the subject) | chroma of the lit part | warmth of the lit part | pixels below L 40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| the upload | 8.7 | 135.9 | 40.8 | 34.8 | 83.9 % |
| the automatic enhancement (result 119) | **23.7** | 163.5 | 45.0 | 37.6 | 80.4 % |
| the owner's hand edit of that result | **1.2** | 151.8 | 48.7 | 41.5 | 83.9 % |

* The enhancement lifted the floor of a deliberately dark photograph by 15 levels (a low-light step, "brightened the dark subject", although the analyzer had already read `dark_scene_not_a_defect`).
  The hand edit put it back, a little deeper than the upload, and gave the subject a little more contrast and colour.
* The model-backed sliders (noise reduction, deblur, face restoration) changed that render by **0.77 of 255** on average (0.0 would be nothing, 22.6 is the whole edit): the look is the tone sliders.

## What Auto does now (`enhancement/auto_edit.py`)

It measures the photograph, names its nature, and solves the sliders for a target with the renderer's own formulas (`tone.py`: brightness adds 0.35 per unit to L, contrast scales about the median
by 0.005 per unit):

* **`low_key`**: half the pixels near black (L below 40), a lit subject (5 % of the pixels above L 60, 95th percentile above 90). Its black floor is compared with the **unenhanced photograph**; only a
  floor the enhancement lifted by more than 3 levels is put back (to 15 % of the original floor: the owner's 1.2 / 8.7), with brightness `-(floor - target) / 0.35`, shadows at 0.28 of that (the
  owner's -18 / -65), contrast so the subject's 95th percentile reaches 155 (the owner's 152), and saturation 14 / warmth 9 scaled down where the lit part has less colour. A floor the enhancement did
  not lift (a naturally dark grey background) is **not** crushed, and without the unenhanced photograph the exposure is left alone.
* **everything else (`balanced`)**: the earlier recipe, exactly (tests assert equality), which never changes the exposure or the colour.
* Only tone sliders are set; the model-backed ones stay manual.

For the owner's photograph (`scripts/compare_auto_edit.py`, tone sliders only):

| slider | Auto | the owner's edit |
|---|---|---|
| brightness | -64.1 | -65 |
| contrast | 20.1 | 18 |
| shadows | -17.9 | -18 |
| highlights | 0 | 1 |
| saturation | 14 | 14 |
| warmth | 9 | 9 |

Floor 0.9 / 0.9, subject 153.6 / 151.8, chroma 49.5 / 49.4; the two renders differ by **0.23 of 255** on average (the enhanced result and the owner's edit differ by 22.6). Looked at side by side
they are not distinguishable.

## On the rest of the owner's library (54 distinct enhanced results, read-only)

6 are `low_key`: the owner's photograph in two versions get brightness about -62 to -64 and the same colour and contrast; **three** (one of 5500 x 3616 px) have blacks "as photographed" and get no
brightness change (without the unenhanced photograph, the first run of this recipe would have taken the 5500 px one to brightness -70, which is why it uses it); one has no lifted floor and gets only
contrast and colour. The other 48 are `balanced` and get what they got before.

## Not verified, and not done

* **One photograph, one hand edit.** The constants marked "owner" (black ratio 0.15, subject 155, shadows 0.28, saturation 14, warmth 9) were read from it. Whether they are right for other dark
  portraits is untested; `scripts/compare_auto_edit.py --source ... --baseline ... --edit '{...}'` compares Auto with any other hand edit in one command, and more edits are what would tune them.
* A photograph that is dark everywhere (nothing lit) is **not** lifted and a bright one is **not** darkened by Auto: there was no edit to say what is wanted. (I first wrote rules for both and a
  muted/garish saturation nudge; the nudge changed 13 of 21 ordinary photographs, none of it was backed by evidence, so they were removed.)
* The **automatic enhancement itself** still lifts the floor of such a photograph (23.7 against 8.7); Auto undoes it on request. Not lifting it in the first place is a change to the pipeline for
  every upload and is left as a decision.
* Noise reduction / deblur / face restoration are not chosen by Auto (their share of the owner's edit was 0.8 of 255).
* Checked with synthetic photographs in CI (`enhancement/test_auto_edit.py`: the nature, the formulas against the renderer, the guards, the other intents) and on the owner's library as above; the
  editor screen itself was not run.
