# Places: how well the offline rule names where a photograph was taken

Measured 2026-10-22 with the real GeoNames `cities1000` snapshot (171,572 places; checksum in `docs/model-licenses/location-gazetteer.md`) and the shipped rule in `places/gazetteer.py`.
Raw result: `2026-10-22-places.json`.

## What was measured

40 positions of well-known places, 38 on land with a place a person would name and 2 with no land or settlement at all. For each, the rule was asked for a place and the answer was judged
**right** if its country was correct and its name was one of the names I accepted for that position (written by me beforehand from general knowledge; the coordinates are approximate, to
about 100 m). It is a check of the naming rule on landmarks, not of GPS accuracy, not of photographs, and not of rural or unusual places.

## The rule, and how it was chosen

| Rule tried on the same 40 positions | Right |
|---|---|
| The nearest listed place | 21 of 40 (it picks neighbourhoods: "Paris 16 Passy", "Downtown Dubai", "Times Square", "Celio") |
| Nearest, never a part of a town or an abandoned/historical place | 28 of 40 |
| **Shipped:** the same, and when the nearest place has fewer than 20,000 people and a place of 20,000 or more is within 15 km, use the bigger one | **34 of 40**, or 33 with unrounded positions (see below) |
| The same with 50,000 people within 20 km | 30 of 40 |
| The same with 100,000 people within 25 km | 31 of 40 |

**The rule was chosen on these same 40 positions, so 34 of 40 is optimistic for other places.** There was no separate test set.

## Result of the shipped rule

34 of 40 right. The 2 positions with no settlement within 50 km (mid-Atlantic, the Rub' al Khali desert) correctly got **no place**. The 6 others were in the right country and the right
city, but named by a district or a neighbouring area instead of the city:

| Position | Named |
|---|---|
| Eiffel Tower | Paris 16 Passy |
| Tokyo Tower | Minato City |
| Christ the Redeemer | Botafogo (Rio de Janeiro) |
| Great Pyramid of Giza | Kirdasah (Giza) |
| Petronas Towers | Bukit Bintang (Kuala Lumpur) |
| Lalbagh Fort | Azimpur (Dhaka) |

A seventh position is a coin toss: the Burj Khalifa is about 13.8 km from two big places, "Dubai" and "Warisan"; the shipped code rounds positions to about 110 m for its cache and names it "Dubai" (counted right above), while the exact position names it "Warisan". So the honest figure is 33 to 34 of 40.

This is the known limit: in very large cities the data lists districts, and a photograph there may be named by its district. It never named a place in the wrong country in this set.

## Speed

Loading the file into memory: 0.28 s, once per process (about 25 MB). A lookup that is not cached: 2.8 ms; repeated positions (rounded to about 110 m) are cached. Placing 20,000 photographs in a
backfill is therefore about a minute. Measured on one Apple M1; SQLite and PostgreSQL are not involved in this step.

## Not measured

Real photographs with real GPS (the GPS parsing is tested on files the tests write, in both hemispheres and in JPEG and PNG); phones that write unusual EXIF; rural, border and island
positions; places outside the data; a second dataset to check the rule against.
